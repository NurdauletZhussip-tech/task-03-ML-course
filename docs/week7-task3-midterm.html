Task 3 — Midterm Project: from a dataset to a model

Weeks 1–6: the whole supervised pipeline (clustering, week 7, is not assessed) Graded — Task 3, 50 points, project with defence Due: Sunday 18 October 2026, 23:59 (Canvas time, UTC+5) Instructor: Baubek Ukibassov

In Tasks 1 and 2 the steps were laid out for you. Here they are not. You get a dataset and a business question, and you hand in a trained model. Everything in between is yours to decide, do and defend: what to look at, what to clean, what to compare, what to trust. This page gives you only the outline of the pipeline and the rules for what you submit.

The situation. A Portuguese bank phones its clients to sell a term deposit. The call centre can phone only 10 % of the clients on a list in one campaign wave. Your model must rank the clients so that the 10 % who get a call are as likely as possible to say yes. The scoring is done before any call is made.
The data are the UCI Bank Marketing database, file bank-additional-full.csv (Moro, Rita & Cortez, 2014; CC BY 4.0): calls made between May 2008 and November 2010. It is a teaching dataset; do not draw conclusions about real banks or real clients from it. After the removal of 12 exact duplicate rows it was split at random into 32 940 rows in train.csv (with the answer y, 11.3 % “yes”) and 8 236 rows in test.csv (without the answer). The answers for test.csv are kept by the instructor and are never shown.
This is individual work. Use the last 4 digits of your student ID as your random seed for every split and every random model, so that your numbers differ from a classmate's. The data are the same for everyone, so identical notebooks will be looked at, and the defence (below) is where you show that the work is yours. You are graded on method and on whether your conclusions follow from your numbers.
Use what was taught in weeks 1–6: preprocessing, logistic regression, KNN, decision trees, random forests, model evaluation, SVM. Clustering (week 7) is not part of this task. Models must come from scikit-learn. You may read any documentation; the code you submit must be code you can explain.

1Blueprint of the pipeline

Eight stages. For each one, the questions you must be able to answer. How you answer them, with which code and which plots, is your decision. There are no code snippets and no expected values in this handout.

Stage 1 · Frame the problem Framing & EDA

  • What exactly is being predicted, for whom, and what does the 10 % call-centre capacity change about which metric you should optimise?
  • test.csv does not have the same columns as train.csv. Which differ, why, and what does that mean for the model you are allowed to build?

Stage 2 · Understand the data Framing & EDA

  • How imbalanced is the target? What does a classifier that always answers “no” score on accuracy?
  • Which columns contain values that mean “missing” or “not applicable” rather than a real measurement? Count them.
  • What order are the rows in, what could that order mean, and does your split respect it?
  • Which features look related to the answer, and by how much? A plot without a number next to it does not count.

Stage 3 · Design the validation Model selection

  • Put a hold-out of your own aside from train.csv, and do not touch it until the last stage. Everything else (comparison, tuning) happens by cross-validation on the remaining part.
  • Which splitter, and why? How large is the spread between folds, and what does that spread say about differences between models?

Stage 4 · Preprocess inside a pipeline Preprocessing

  • Every step that learns something from data (filling, scaling, encoding) must be fitted on training folds only. How do you guarantee that, and how would you show a reader that it holds?
  • Decide, per column type, how to encode it and how to treat the “missing” values you found. Justify at least one decision with cross-validation numbers against an alternative.
  • The saved pipeline must accept the raw columns of test.csv with no manual step before it.

Stage 5 · Baselines Baselines

  • A majority-class baseline and one simple model, on the same folds, with mean and standard deviation. These are the bar every later model has to clear.

Stage 6 · Compare models and tune Model selection

  • At least three different model families from the course, in the same pipeline, on the same folds.
  • Tune hyper-parameters by cross-validation on the training part only; state the search space you used.
  • Does handling the class imbalance help? Show the numbers with and without.
  • Which differences are larger than the fold spread, and which are not? Do not declare a winner inside the noise.

Stage 7 · Final evaluation, once Final evaluation

  • Fit the chosen pipeline and score your own hold-out, once. Report ROC-AUC and average precision with their curves.
  • Turn the score into a decision with the capacity rule: choose the cutoff on your hold-out so that the top 10 % of clients are called. Report precision, recall and the lift over phoning clients at random, and read the confusion matrix in plain language for the call-centre manager.
  • The split of this data is random, but the real use is “train on the past, call in the future”. Say in a few sentences how this flatters your numbers.

Stage 8 · Save and describe the model Submission, Reproducibility

  • Refit on all of train.csv, save the pipeline, and write the predictions for test.csv (exact format in the next section).
  • Write REPORT.md, one to two pages, a model card: purpose, data, pipeline, metric and results, limits and the ways it can fail in use, what you would monitor after launch.

Build the pipeline from scikit-learn parts, or put your helper code in a .py file in the repository root and import it in the notebook, so that the pickle refers to that file. 3The defence

After the deadline you defend the project in person, one student at a time. You will be asked to walk through your pipeline without reading the notebook line by line, to explain a result and say whether it is inside the noise, and to make a small change live (for example a different cutoff, a different metric, or one feature removed), saying what you expect to happen before you run it. The instructor announces the schedule in Canvas. A submission without a defence cannot receive the defence points, and a defence that shows the work is not yours can change the points given for the other parts.
